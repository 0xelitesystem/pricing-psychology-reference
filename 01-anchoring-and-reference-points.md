# Anchoring and reference points

The single most reliable finding in pricing is that judgments are relative. A price means little on its own; it means something against a reference point. The first number a person sees becomes that reference, and every later number is judged as higher or lower than it. This is anchoring, and it operates whether or not the anchor is relevant.

## How the anchor works

When someone encounters a high number first, later prices feel reasonable by comparison; when they encounter a low number first, the same later prices feel expensive. The anchor sets the scale. This is why a product page often shows a premium option before the one most people buy: the premium price is the anchor, and the middle option reads as sensible next to it. The order of presentation, not just the prices, shapes the decision.

## Reference points are built, not fixed

A buyer arrives with some existing sense of what a thing should cost, formed by past purchases and competitors. But that reference is soft and can be shifted by what you show. A strikethrough original price next to a current price makes the current price read as a saving against the anchor of the original. A comparison to a more expensive alternative reframes your price as the affordable choice. The reference point is partly yours to set, which is power that can be used fairly or unfairly.

## The risk of a dishonest anchor

The line is crossed when the anchor is fake. A strikethrough price that was never the real price, an inflated original invented only to make the discount look larger, or a comparison to a competitor product that does not actually exist at that price, these manipulate the reference rather than inform it. Many jurisdictions regulate exactly this, requiring that a was price was genuinely charged. Beyond the legal risk, a buyer who later discovers the anchor was invented loses trust in everything else you say.

## Honest anchoring

The fair use of anchoring is to give a true and relevant reference. Show your premium tier first because it genuinely exists. Compare to a real alternative the buyer is actually weighing. State a real previous price. The mechanism, that the first number shapes the rest, is neutral; what makes it honest is whether the anchor is real. Used this way, anchoring helps a buyer place your offer correctly rather than tricking them into misjudging it.

## Why it matters for your own pricing

Anchoring also affects how you set prices, not just how buyers read them. If you anchor your own thinking to a competitor who is underpricing, you may set your price too low and never recover the margin. The same relativity that moves buyers moves sellers. Being aware that your sense of the right price is itself anchored, often to whatever you saw first, is the start of setting a price from your costs and value rather than from someone else's number.
